Germany's telling people to stockpile food and water…

Hurricane Katrina, Sandy, Harvey, Helene.
And I'm telling you, the agencies are moving in a way I've never seen before.
Not faster. Quieter.
We're not allowed to say certain things in public briefings anymore…
We're not allowed to use certain timeframes…
But the internal memos? They don't say 72 hours.
They say extended grid failure. Prolonged service disruption. 90-day operational independence.
And every single one of those memos has the same line buried in the logistics section.
Hypothermia becomes the primary threat within 72 hours of winter grid loss.
Let me tell you what that actually means, because I've seen it in every major cold-weather disaster I've ever worked.
Your house runs on one thing: heat.
And heat needs electricity or fuel.
When the grid goes down in winter, your house becomes a freezer.
Within 6 hours, interior temperatures start dropping.
Within 24 hours, your house equalizes with the temperature outside.

Texas, February 2021. Four million people lost power. Temperatures in the teens.
246 people died.
Most of them from hypothermia.
In their own homes.

Families who did everything right still fell apart by day 3.
They had the generator…

They had propane heaters…
But propane heaters need ventilation and families died from carbon monoxide trying to stay warm.
They had blankets…
But blankets don't stop your core temperature from dropping when you're sitting in 19-degree air for 48 hours.
So they tried to improvise.
Running cars in garages until the carbon monoxide got them.
Burning furniture in fireplaces that hadn't been inspected in years and starting house fires.
Huddling under every blanket they owned while their body temperature dropped degree by degree.
By day 3, I've seen families trying to decide whether to stay in a freezing house or leave with nowhere to go.
I've seen elderly people who couldn't get warm no matter what they tried.
I've seen parents watching their kids shiver under six blankets knowing nothing they were doing was enough.
After Texas, the after-action reports all said the same thing.
The deaths clustered in the first 72 hours.
Before response could reach people.
In that window where they were on their own.
And the cause of death was almost always the same.
Hypothermia.
Not because help didn't come.
Because help couldn't come fast enough.
And here's the part that made me break protocol and write this.
2 months ago, I was in a supply coordination meeting.
We were reviewing the federal emergency stockpile.
Millions of MREs. Pallets of water bottles. Generators. Medical supplies.
And someone asked: "What about emergency heat for extended winter grid-down scenarios?"
The answer was: "That's not a federal priority. Individuals are responsible for their own heat backup."
Let me say that again.
If the grid goes down for 90 days in winter, FEMA is not coming to your house with a heat solution.
You're on your own.

A TerraShell emergency bivouac for every person in my house.
No power. No fuel. No installation.

Garage stayed at 28 degrees the whole time.
I stayed warm the whole time.
